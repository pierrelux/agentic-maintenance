ACTION SUBMITTED: replace_bearing (single work order, submitted; status confirmed by the plant).

DIAGNOSIS: Progressive degradation of the P-201 thrust bearing (increased friction from wear or lubricant film breakdown). Oil and radial bearing are secondarily elevated, so a cooler contribution is possible but not the primary cause. Ambient, RTD-only drift and a cooler-only fault were each judged unlikely.

KEY EVIDENCE (all temperatures as differences above room ambient, which was about 18 C at the alarm):
- Thrust bearing (Tt) rose from about 45 C above ambient on 10-14 (manual spec: +44 C) to about 54 C at the alarm on 10-16 10:44, with the rise accelerating over the last 4 h. Alarm value 73.0 C, limit 72 C.
- Oil and radial bearing (Tr) stayed flat relative to ambient (oil about 28-30 C, spec +29; Tr about 37 C, spec +38) for roughly 40 h while Tt climbed. They only rose in the last ~4 h, reaching about +7 C (oil) and +6 C (Tr) above spec.
- Because oil and Tr are also elevated, a single-sensor RTD offset cannot explain the event. Because the differences to ambient changed for all three sensors, an ambient effect is excluded.
- Speed step test (90 %, 45 min): Tt fell 6.9 C (72.83 to 65.91 C). The manual's nominal reference is about 6 C, and a larger drop indicates increased friction. Oil fell 2.3 C and Tr fell 4.0 C. Ambient was rising about 0.5 C/h, so the drop corrected for ambient is slightly larger still.
- Maintenance log 2026-10-07 already noted a slight rise on the thrust side (within limits).
- The last few hours show oil rising first with Tt following, which is consistent with some cooler fouling. That does not explain the 40 h Tt-only rise, so it is treated as secondary.

TESTS RUN AND WHY:
- Speed step test at 90 % for 45 min (production cost 1,348 CAD). This is the manual's only discriminator between friction and sensor offset. It was worth running because the replace-or-not decision hinges on it.
- No technician inspection. Its cost is about 1,800 and 8 h of delay. A normal vibration reading would not exclude bearing distress (the manual says vibration detects it in most but not all cases), so it would not change the replace decision.
- No RTD or cooler test. The oil and Tr evidence already argues against an RTD-only fault, and the cooler cannot be the sole cause given the 40 h Tt trend.
- Cost reasoning: replacing the bearing costs about 28,000, while a bearing failure in service costs about 260,000 (18 h outage) plus about 175,000 if the oil overheats. Cleaning the cooler alone would only be the cheaper option if the bearing were under about 10 % likely, which the evidence does not support.
